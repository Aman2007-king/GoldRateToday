Understanding Making Charges and Wastage

Buying gold in India involves more than just the market rate. When a piece of jewellery is crafted, two additional costs are added: Making Charges and Wastage. Making charges represent the artisan's labor cost. These vary by complexity—simple chains might have a 5-8% charge, while intricate bridal sets can go up to 25%. "Wastage" refers to the gold lost during melting and soldering. While modern tech reduces loss, jewellers often charge a percentage for this. Always ask for a transparent "Price Breakup" before paying.

How India Buys Gold Jewellery

India remains the global heart of gold consumption. Cultural buying is driven by the Hindu Calendar, specifically festivals like Dhanteras and Akshaya Tritiya. Historically, gold was bought for security (Streedhan), but today it is an asset class. Southern India accounts for nearly 40% of the country's demand, followed by the West. Interestingly, rural India buys more gold than urban centers, using it as a "private bank" to hedge against inflation.

What is BIS Hallmark & HUID?

To prevent purity fraud, the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) provides hallmarking. Since 2021, the HUID (Hallmark Unique Identification) has become mandatory. This is a 6-digit alphanumeric code laser-etched on your gold. It allows you to track the gold's purity through the BIS Care App. Never buy gold without the three vital marks: the BIS logo, the purity grade (e.g., 22K916), and the unique HUID code.

Popular Gold Schemes by Jewellers

Most top-tier jewellers offer monthly saving schemes. You pay a fixed amount for 10 or 11 months, and the jeweller pays the last installment as a "bonus." These schemes, like Tanishq's Golden Harvest, help lock in prices and offer discounts on making charges. They are ideal for planned purchases like weddings but remember: these are not cash investments, they must be redeemed for jewellery.

How Gold Prices Move in India

Gold prices in India are influenced by three major factors:

  • International Rates: The London Bullion Market (LBMA) sets the global standard in USD.
  • Currency Exchange: If the Indian Rupee (INR) falls against the US Dollar, gold becomes more expensive locally.
  • Import Duty: Changes in government taxes directly impact the daily rate you see at showrooms.
